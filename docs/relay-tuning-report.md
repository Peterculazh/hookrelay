# Milestone 12 relay tuning results

Milestone 12 was verified on 2026-10-01. A 100-event batch every second replaced
the Kubernetes deployment's ten-second publication schedule. All six VPS
comparison runs passed, with traffic generated by k6 on the local PC.
The runs accepted and delivered 4506 events, with exactly one successful
HTTP 204 attempt and one receiver effect each. No iterations were dropped.
The local ten-scenario regression suite and a 60-second steady profile passed,
along with 82 unit tests, lint, type checking, and controller checks.
Numeric results and release/restoration evidence are in
[relay-tuning-results.json](relay-tuning-results.json).

In the 15-second pressure comparison, delivery p95 improved from
**15.39 seconds to 1.42 seconds**.
Peak unpublished outbox fell from **201 to 21**.
The tuned repeat passed too. Whole-run rates include observer startup, admission,
and draining; they are not maximum capacity measurements.

For the 60-second profiles, the steady window begins 20 seconds after the k6
process launches and ends with the last observation before it exits. Generator
startup/exit overhead, polling, and network delay are included. Completed-delivery
deltas are divided by the actual recorded observation-window duration.

The legacy schedule accumulated unpublished work while admitting 20 events/s.
The one-second schedule kept pace near the offered rate through the measured

**20.10 completed deliveries/s** with
concurrency one, confirming the final deployed configuration at this offered
rate and duration. No maximum sustainable or production capacity claim is made.

These sampled maxima can miss bursts and may come from different timestamps.
Raw database activity/lock waits, queue depth, Node metrics, and Pod samples
remain in the evidence directories recorded in the JSON. Database polling and
the controlled receiver proxy add work to the VPS. Faster idle publication also
performs more empty batch queries: roughly one per second instead of one per ten
seconds, before missed ticks or active-batch skipping.

API latency includes network, SSH, Kubernetes forwarding, and response-body time.
Delivery latency uses the PC's monotonic acknowledgment-to-observation clock,
including database polling and return-network delay. Polling may miss short
outbox peaks. Individual maximum observation intervals are retained. Sixty
seconds is enough to expose the previous publication limit at 20 events/s;
longer endurance and higher-rate saturation tests remain future work.
